⚡ Caching, Explained

A cache is a small, fast memory that keeps copies of things you used recently — so you don't have to fetch them from the slow place again. Click through the demos below.

1The big idea

Fast storage is small and expensive. Slow storage is huge and cheap. A cache puts a bit of the fast stuff in front of the slow stuff.

📚 Analogy: You're studying. The book on your desk is instant. The bookshelf takes a minute. The library takes an hour. You keep the books you use most on your desk — that's caching.

Three ways to keep it fresh: ① TTL – let it expire automatically (simple, may be stale until then). ② Explicit invalidation – delete/update on write (fresh, but you must remember to). ③ Versioned keys – app.v42.js never goes stale; a new version is a new key.

8Classic cache problems

Caches are great until they fail in these ways.

🐘 Cache stampede

A hot key expires; 1,000 requests miss at once and all hit the DB.
Fix: lock / single-flight, refresh early, jittered TTLs.

🏔️ Cache avalanche

Many keys expire at the same moment (or the cache dies) → DB flood.
Fix: randomize TTLs, replicas, circuit breakers.

🕳️ Cache penetration

Requests for keys that don't exist always miss, always hit the DB.
Fix: cache "null" results, Bloom filter.

🧟 Stale data

Cache says $10, DB says $12.
Fix: shorter TTL, invalidate on write, versioned keys.

🔥 Hot key

One key gets all traffic, overloading one cache node.
Fix: local in-process copy, replicate the key.

❄️ Cold start

Empty cache after deploy/restart = all misses.
Fix: pre-warm with popular keys.

9Cheat sheet: patterns at a glance

PatternHow it worksUse when
Cache-aside (lazy)App checks cache → on miss loads DB → fills cacheMost common default; read-heavy
Read-throughCache itself loads from DB on missYou want simpler app code
Write-throughWrite to cache and DB togetherConsistency matters
Write-backWrite to cache, flush to DB laterWrite-heavy, can tolerate small loss
Refresh-aheadReload popular keys before they expireAvoid latency spikes
MemoizationCache a function's result by its argumentsPure, expensive computations
Golden rules: ① Cache only what is read often & changes rarely. ② Always decide how it expires. ③ Measure your hit ratio — a cache with a low hit ratio is just extra latency.
